# Automatic revocation of exemption

**What it checks.** Whether the organization appears on the IRS Automatic Revocation of
Exemption List, and if so, whether it has been reinstated.

**What automatic revocation is.** Three consecutive years without filing a Form 990,
990-EZ, or 990-N revokes tax-exempt status automatically, by operation of law. There is no
hearing, no discretion, and often no notice that anybody at the organization actually reads.
A revoked organization is ineligible for federal grants and cannot receive tax-deductible
contributions until it is reinstated.

**The trap this check exists to avoid.** **Being on the list does not mean being revoked
today.** Organizations that have been reinstated stay on the published list permanently,
with a reinstatement date recorded alongside the revocation. In the 2026-08-11 file,
181,259 of 1,246,171 rows carry a reinstatement date — roughly one in seven. Reading list
membership alone as "revoked" would tell every one of them they cannot apply for federal
money. This check reads the dates.

A second case it handles: an organization can be revoked, reinstated, and revoked **again**.
19,136 organizations have more than one entry. Current status comes from the most recent
revocation, not from whichever row happens to come first.

**What to do.** If the exemption is currently revoked, reinstatement is by Form 1023 or
1023-EZ. Retroactive reinstatement has deadlines — see IRS Revenue Procedure 2014-11.
