# Single audit

**What it checks.** Whether the organization may be required to have a single audit, based
on federal awards expended during its fiscal year.

**This is a screen, not an answer.** The figure that decides it — federal awards expended —
is not published in the datasets this tool draws on. What the check can tell you is which
threshold applies to your fiscal year, and where to find the number.

**The threshold, and the part people get wrong.**

- **$750,000** for fiscal years beginning **before** 1 October 2024
- **$1,000,000** for fiscal years beginning **on or after** 1 October 2024

It keys on when the fiscal year **began**, not when it ended and not when you filed. An
organization whose year ran 1 July 2024 to 30 June 2025 files well into the $1,000,000 era
and is still measured against $750,000.

**Three things that are easy to leave out of the calculation.**

1. It counts what was **expended**, not what was received. Money drawn down this year
   against an award made two years ago counts this year.
2. It counts **pass-through subawards**. Federal money received through a state agency, a
   university, or a larger nonprofit is still federal money expended, and this is the part
   most often missed.
3. It is the total across all federal sources, not per award.

**What to do.** Your Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards is where the number lives.
If you are near the line, talk to your auditor before the fiscal year ends rather than after.
