# Years since filing

**What it checks.** How long it has been since the most recent annual return on record, and
therefore how close the organization is to automatic revocation.

**Why this is the most useful line in the report.** Revocation is not a judgment call. It
happens on a three-year counter: three consecutive years without filing a Form 990, 990-EZ,
or 990-N and the exemption is gone by operation of law. Nobody publishes the distance to
that cliff, which is exactly why it is worth computing.

**What the statuses mean.** Zero or one year is fine. Two years is a warning — one more
missed year and the exemption goes. Three or more is reported as a problem, and the
organization may already be on the next revocation posting.

**Three guards, because this line can do harm.** Organizations with no filing requirement
are never flagged. Group subordinates covered by a central organization's group return are
never flagged. And where the only available signal is the Business Master File's processed
tax period, the check caps its severity at a warning and says why — that figure lags real
filing by up to a year, and reporting it as delinquency would accuse an organization that
filed on time.

**What to do.** If returns are genuinely missing, file them. Reinstatement after revocation
is considerably more work than filing late.
